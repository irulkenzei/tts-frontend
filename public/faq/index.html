FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What does Narator AI do?

It helps users clone voices, generate narrated audio, transcribe video into subtitles, turn documents into audiobook-style narration, and draft scripts with an AI Assistant -- all in one workflow.

Can I clone my own voice?

Yes. You should only upload audio you own or have explicit permission to use.

What kind of source audio works best?

Clear audio from one speaker, low background noise, and a stable speaking pace generally produce better results.

Why does my result sound inconsistent?

Short samples, noisy recordings, clipped speech, or multiple speakers in one file can lower output quality.

Can Narator AI generate subtitles for my videos?

Yes. Upload a short video (up to 30 seconds) and Narator AI will transcribe the audio and generate a downloadable .srt or .vtt subtitle file.

Can I turn a book or document into an audiobook?

Yes. Import an EPUB or DOCX file and Narator AI will generate audiobook-style narration chapter by chapter, using the voice of your choice.

What is the AI Assistant?

It's a built-in writing helper that can suggest scripts, narration ideas, and dialogue. You can choose which AI provider powers it; some providers are available to Pro subscribers only.

Do I need an internet connection?

Yes. Voice processing, transcription, document narration, account features, and the AI Assistant all depend on network access.

Where should I go if something fails?

Start with the Troubleshoot page. If the issue continues, use Contact.

How does Narator AI distinguish between a person's voice and the spoken language?

Narator AI uses a separation approach between Speaker Embedding ("Who") and Target Language ("What"). The model processes these two inputs independently to generate audio that combines the vocal identity from your sample with the linguistic rules of the input text.

What is "Speaker Embedding" and what is its function?

"Speaker Embedding" is the process where the model analyzes your voice sample (typically around 6-10 seconds long) to capture vocal characteristics. This data includes the timbre (voice color), pitch, and the unique speaking style of an individual.

What is the role of "Target Language" in this process?

The "Target Language" acts as an instruction for the model regarding which language to speak. The model uses this data to determine the phonetics, rhythm, and accent appropriate for that language, regardless of the language used in the original voice sample.

If I use an English voice sample to speak German text, what language will be generated?

The result will be German. Although the voice or vocal identity generated is that of the English speaker, the model will apply German phonetic rules to speak your text.

Will the generated voice have a foreign accent?

Yes, this may occur. Because the model attempts to force the vocal characteristics of the original sample into the rules of the target language, it may sometimes sound like there is a slight "foreign accent." However, in terms of grammar and phonetic accuracy, the output will remain consistent with the target language you selected.
